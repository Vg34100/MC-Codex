# Quest writing style

Use a casual, direct voice with natural contractions. Give the player something useful to read, with a little personality where it fits. Keep it close to how the user talks, without copying typos, adding forced slang or putting profanity everywhere.

- Explain what to do and why it might be useful. Keep instructions accurate to the actual objective.
- Avoid grand narration, vague life lessons and personifying ordinary materials. The user specifically rejected “Stone remembers every strike.”
- Do not invent nearby terrain, a time of day, NPCs or events that may not exist in the player's world.
- Keep implementation jargon out of player text: no “native advancement,” “ordinary vanilla objective” or editor demonstrations in the story.
- Completion lines can be brief. They do not all need a dramatic payoff or the same “Nice!” template.
- Keep titles and choices easy to understand: “Set up camp” / “Keep exploring.”

For example: “Wooden tools don't last long. Mine some stone and grab the cobblestone for a stone pickaxe.”

The supplied examples follow this style. Existing player-authored quest text must be preserved when updating them.
